# Discovery

Discovery is the first paid engagement after a successful Fit Check. The goal is to learn about the client's business goals and uncover how a custom software product could serve those goals.

## Format

Discovery is a focused working session where the client and I talk through the business, the people the product needs to serve, and the shape of the software that could support those goals. It can happen live, async in a shared doc, or as a mix of both. Existing artifacts are useful context: notes, decks, sketches, research, spreadsheets, prototypes, current workflows, or anything else that helps explain the business and product idea.

Useful prompts:

- What business goal does this product need to serve?
- What changes if this product works?
- Who needs to use it, buy it, approve it, or operate it?
- What does the first useful version need to make possible?
- What would make the first release too small to matter?

What I cover:

- What kinds of software shape could serve the business goal.
- Which parts are straightforward, which parts are risky, and which parts need more evidence.
- How the product could be released in useful stages.
- What should be captured as product scope, release targets, open questions, or deliberate no-s.

## Outcomes

The outcomes of Discovery are concrete artifacts that make the product direction easier to discuss, scope, and revise.

**Business goals:**

The business goal the product is meant to serve. If there are several goals in play, the work should identify the primary goal and the supporting goals that affect product shape, audience, release timing, or scope.

**Product audiences:**

The Product Audiences the product is meant to serve. These are the people or organizations the product needs to work for, not necessarily the same groups that will get access to each release.

**Product type:**

What kind of software product this is becoming, and how custom software could serve the business goal. The description should be concrete enough to distinguish the product from adjacent ideas before the specific feature list is defined.

**Feature list:**

A high-level feature list that names the major capabilities the product appears to need. The list should be specific enough to support scope conversation, but broad enough to leave detailed planning for later work.

**Release targets:**

The Release Targets that make sense for the product. Each Release Target names the Audience Profile and Access Boundary for a meaningful released state.

**Planning & Design scope:**

An agreement on the concrete artifacts that Planning & Design will produce. Included here so the scope and expectations for that engagement are clear before it begins. Common examples:

- Wireframes of key user flows
- Hi fidelity examples of component UI
- A product branding guide
